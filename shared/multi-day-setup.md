## End of Session 1 — start the corrosion jars

**Time needed: 10 minutes.** Do it in the last ten minutes of Day 1, as a teaser rather than an experiment — students have just built a fruit battery from two dissimilar metals, so the jars are the same idea left running.

### What to say

*"You have just made electricity out of two different metals in a liquid. Here is the bad news: this happens whether you want it to or not, anywhere two metals meet water. These three jars are going to sit here all week. Look at them every day, and on Friday you will tell me what happened."*

### The jars

Same 5–10% saltwater in each (25–50 g NaCl per 500 mL), about 100–150 mL per jar.

| Jar | Contents | Expected by Session 5 |
|-----|----------|-----------------------|
| **A** | Iron nail alone | Moderate, even rust — the control |
| **B** | Iron nail in **firm contact** with copper | Noticeably worse rust on the iron |
| **C** | Iron nail in **firm contact** with zinc, or a galvanized nail | Iron largely clean; zinc dulls and powders |

### The three rules that decide whether this works

1. **The metals must physically touch**, tightly — wired, clipped, or twisted together. No electrical contact means no galvanic couple, and jar B ends up indistinguishable from jar A. This is the most common failure by a wide margin.
2. **Fill each jar only half full**, so the metals cross the waterline. Corrosion is fastest where oxygen and water are both plentiful, and that is at the surface.
3. **Leave them open to the air.** The cathodic half-reaction consumes dissolved oxygen; a sealed jar runs out and stalls.

### Checks during the week

- [ ] Day 2 — contacts still firm?
- [ ] Day 3 — photograph all three; differences should be starting
- [ ] Day 4 — contacts still firm? Top up water lost to evaporation
- [ ] Day 5 — move them somewhere everyone can see

Keep the jars visible all week and let students form opinions before Friday. The daily commentary is half the value.

## End of Session 4 — start the derusting cells

**Time needed: the last 8 minutes of Session 4.** Full procedure in [Session 4 experiment.md](../sessions/04-water-electrolysis/experiment.md) Part E.

The short version:

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Electrolyte | Washing soda, 1 tbsp per 250 mL warm water (baking soda acceptable) |
| Cathode (−) | The rusty iron |
| Anode (+) | Sacrificial steel or graphite — **not stainless** |
| Control | An equally rusty nail, same solution, **no power** |
| Power | **Mains DC supply or 5 V USB brick** |
| Ventilation | Jars open, room ventilated |

**The two things that ruin it:**

- **A 9 V alkaline battery.** It holds about 500 mAh; the cell draws a few hundred mA. It is flat in roughly two hours and Session 5 opens on a nail that looks exactly like the control.
- **No control nail.** Alkaline solution alone changes a rusty nail's appearance overnight. Without the unpowered control you cannot show that the electricity did anything.

Photograph the "before". Label every jar.

# Session 4 — Experiment: Water Electrolysis

> **Instructors:** gas ID, ratio systematics, Part E polarity — [instructor.md](instructor.md).

---

## Electrolyte — sodium sulfate (Na₂SO₄)

**Never use table salt (NaCl)** — chloride can produce toxic chlorine gas at the anode.

### Recipe — ~0.1 M Na₂SO₄

| Batch | Na₂SO₄ (anhydrous) | Water |
|-------|--------------------|-------|
| 250 mL (about one U-tube fill) | **~3.5 g** | Fill to 250 mL |
| 500 mL | **~7 g** | Fill to 500 mL |
| 1.0 L | **~14 g** | Fill to 1.0 L |

Stir until dissolved. Label **"0.1 M Na₂SO₄ electrolyte — NO SALT"**.

**Water:** tap water is fine — Na₂SO₄ provides the ions you need. Distilled water is unnecessary here.

**If you only have baking soda as emergency backup:** 1–2 tsp NaHCO₃ per 500 mL works, but the planned electrolyte for this workshop is **sodium sulfate**.

### Volume per station

Fill the U-tube so both arms are mostly full of electrolyte, with liquid well above the lower ends of the graphite electrodes and below the side-arm ports (see Part A). Mix **fresh** electrolyte if someone contaminated a batch with salt.

## Part A — Assemble the U-tube electrolysis cell (25–40 min)

![Hoffman-style U-tube electrolysis apparatus with graphite electrodes](../../assets/figures/session4-hoffman-utube-apparatus.png)

*Figure 1 — Classroom apparatus: glass U-tube on an acrylic stand, graphite electrodes sealed with rubber stoppers, side arms on each limb, red (+) / black (−) leads with spade connectors for a DC supply.*

![Water electrolysis schematic](../../assets/figures/session4-water-electrolysis.svg)

*Figure 2 — Concept: H₂ forms at the cathode (−) at about twice the volume of O₂ at the anode (+). Electrolyte is sodium sulfate — not salt.*

### Materials

- Hoffman-style **glass U-tube** with **side arms** (one per limb), on acrylic stand
- Two **graphite** electrodes with rubber/cork stoppers
- Red (+) and black (−) leads with spade (fork) connectors
- DC power supply or battery pack (typically **6–12 V**; 9 V packs also work if current is adequate)
- **0.1 M Na₂SO₄** electrolyte
- Ruler or markings to compare gas column heights / liquid levels
- Goggles

### Build steps

1. Place the U-tube securely in its acrylic stand.
2. Fill with **Na₂SO₄** electrolyte until both arms are nearly full — liquid should cover the immersed electrode tips and sit **below** the side-arm openings so gas can collect (or exit) there.
3. Insert one graphite electrode through each rubber stopper into each arm. Stoppers should seal the top of each limb.
4. Confirm electrodes do **not** touch each other or short through loose wires.
5. Connect leads:
   - **Black / (−)** → cathode → electrode that will collect **H₂** (faster-filling limb)
   - **Red / (+)** → anode → other electrode (**O₂**)
6. Attach spade connectors to the DC supply (or battery pack). Keep connections **dry**.
7. Side arms: leave open to air for classroom volume comparison, **or** attach collection tubing if your kit includes it. Do not seal both the stopper tops and the side arms airtight with no outlet — pressure must have a path.
8. When current flows, the **faster-rising gas column** (or faster liquid drop) is hydrogen (cathode).

### Pre-run checklist

- [ ] U-tube filled with **Na₂SO₄** — **not salt**
- [ ] Stoppers seated; electrodes immersed and not touching
- [ ] Polarity marked: (−) = H₂ limb, (+) = O₂ limb
- [ ] Side arms open or properly tubed (no overpressure trap)
- [ ] Goggles on
- [ ] Power supply set; leads dry

## Part B — Run and measure (40–65 min)

1. Start the timer when bubbles are clearly visible at both electrodes.
2. Every **2–3 min**, measure gas produced in each arm:
   - Prefer graduated limbs (mL) if marked, **or**
   - Measure **gas column height** or **liquid level drop** (cm) with a ruler (ratio ≈ volume ratio if the two arms match).
3. Continue until a clear difference appears or ~15–20 min have elapsed.

### Gas volume log

| Time (min) | V_H₂ (mL or cm) | V_O₂ (mL or cm) | Ratio V_H₂/V_O₂ |

### Expected result

V_H₂ / V_O₂ ≈ **2** (ideal). Classroom range **1.6–2.2** is a success — discuss solubility and collection errors.

### Observations

- Bubble rate at each electrode
- Cloudiness or electrode crumbling
- Temperature change (optional)

## Part C — Instructor-only pop test (65–75 min)

**Optional demo — not for students**

1. Collect a **tiny** amount of H₂ from the cathode side arm (or a small separate tube).
2. Move away from the main apparatus and faces.
3. Brief flame → soft “pop.”
4. Never ignite sealed or mixed H₂/O₂ containers.
5. Skip if the venue forbids open flame.

## Part E — Start electrolytic rust removal (80–90 min)

![Electrolytic rust removal setup](../../assets/figures/session5-electrolytic-derusting.svg)

*Figure 3 — Start cells now; reveal cleaned nails at opening of Session 5.*

### Derusting electrolyte

| Ingredient | Amount |
|------------|--------|
| **Washing soda** (sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃) preferred | **1 tbsp (~15 g) per ~250 mL** warm water |
| **or baking soda** | **1 tbsp per ~250 mL** warm water |

≈ **0.5–0.6 M** Na₂CO₃ if using washing soda at that kitchen measure — exact molarity is not critical.  
**Do NOT use NaCl.** (Derusting is a separate bath from the Na₂SO₄ U-tube.)

### Setup steps

1. Dissolve washing soda (or baking soda) in warm water; pour into a small jar (~100–250 mL).
2. Clip **rusty iron** nail/hardware to battery **cathode (−)**.
3. Clip **sacrificial steel strip or graphite** to battery **anode (+)**.
4. Immerse both; keep them from touching; confirm gentle bubbling.
5. Label group name; photograph the nail **before**.
6. Place a **control** rusty nail in plain water (no battery).
7. Leave powered overnight per instructor / venue rules.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| No bubbles | Poor contact / weak electrolyte | Check spade connectors; remake ~0.1 M Na₂SO₄ |
| One side only | Single electrode connected | Check both leads and polarity |
| Ratio far from 2:1 | Uneven arms; leaks; late start | Equal fill levels; reseat stoppers; restart timer after steady bubbling |
| Chlorine smell | **Salt used** | Stop; dump; remake **Na₂SO₄** bath |
| Pressure / liquid spray | Side arms sealed with no outlet | Open side arms or attach vent tubing |
| Graphite crumbling | High current / long run | Lower voltage; replace rods |

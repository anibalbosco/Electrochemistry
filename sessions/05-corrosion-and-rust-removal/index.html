Hook: Rust is a battery you did not want. Today we measure the voltage that drives it, make iron rust faster on purpose, protect it, and open the cells that spent the night running the reaction backwards.

Learning outcomes

  • Predict the voltage of a metal pair from the electrochemical series, then measure it and account for the gap
  • Measure the short-circuit current of a galvanic couple and convert it, via Faraday's law, into a corrosion rate in grams per year
  • Explain galvanic corrosion and sacrificial protection, and identify which metal in a pair is at risk
  • Interpret the overnight electrolytic rust removal cells started in Session 4
  • Place all five sessions on a single axis: spontaneous chemistry making electricity, or electricity forcing chemistry

Key reactions

Rusting, as two half-reactions happening on the same piece of iron:

  Anodic sites:   Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻
  Cathodic sites: O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻

  Fe²⁺ is then further oxidized and precipitates as hydrated iron oxides — rust.

Galvanic couple (Fe touching Cu in saltwater):
  Iron becomes the anode of a real cell and corrodes faster.

Sacrificial protection (Fe touching Zn):
  Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ instead. Iron is now the cathode and is protected.

Electrolytic derusting:
  A power supply forces the iron to be the cathode. Oxide is reduced and lifted; H₂ evolves.

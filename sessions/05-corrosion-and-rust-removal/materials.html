Session 5 — Materials

Session 5

Part A — The reveal (cells started in Session 4)

  • [ ] The overnight derusting cells, still powered this morning — check before students arrive
  • [ ] The unpowered control nail, in the same solution, clearly labeled
  • [ ] Printed or on-screen "before" photos
  • [ ] Forceps or gloves
  • [ ] Access to running water for rinsing
  • [ ] Stiff brush or wire brush, and paper towels
  • [ ] Tray to work over — the sludge stains

Part B — Metal-pair cells

Per group

  • [ ] Small jar, ~100 mL
  • [ ] Saltwater 5–10% w/v (25–50 g NaCl per 500 mL), labeled "corrosion demos only"
  • [ ] Copper strip or heavy wire
  • [ ] Iron nail or steel strip
  • [ ] Zinc strip or galvanized nail
  • [ ] Aluminium strip or heavy foil
  • [ ] Multimeter with both DC volts and DC mA
  • [ ] Alligator clips
  • [ ] Sandpaper or steel wool
  • [ ] Paper towels
  • [ ] Calculator
  • [ ] Printed E° table with the predictions column blank — experiment.md Part B1

Optional but worth having

  • [ ] Magnesium ribbon — the biggest voltage of the set and a good contrast with aluminium
  • [ ] A second nail already sanded to bare metal, for comparison

Part C — Corrosion jars

  • [ ] Jars A, B and C, started in Session 1 — see shared/multi-day-setup.md
  • [ ] A photograph of well-developed jars, as a fallback if this year's underperform

Part D and capstone

  • [ ] Printed week-comparison tables
  • [ ] Printed capstone worksheets — lecture.md §5
  • [ ] Photos of sacrificial anodes: a ship's hull, a water-heater rod

Instructor display items

  • [ ] A galvanized nail and a plain iron nail, side by side, to pass around
  • [ ] Activity series written large, to stay on the board all session

Explicitly excluded

  • [ ] ~~NaCl in the derusting bath~~ — washing soda or baking soda only
  • [ ] ~~Stainless steel anodes~~ — can release chromium compounds into the bath

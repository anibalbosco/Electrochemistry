Session 5 — Preparation

Session 5

Check this first, this morning

  • [ ] Are the derusting cells still powered? Touch the supply, look for bubbling, confirm the nail is dark rather than orange. If the power died in the night, read the fallback below before class rather than during it.
  • [ ] Is the control nail there, unpowered, in the same solution? Without it there is no experiment.
  • [ ] Can you find the before photos? Have them open and ready to display.

Session 4 handoff (the critical dependency)

These cells must be running before students leave Session 4:

Item Detail
Electrolyte Washing soda preferred; baking soda acceptable — never NaCl
Cathode (−) Rusty iron
Anode (+) Sacrificial steel or graphite (not stainless)
Control Equally rusty nail, same solution, no power
Power Mains supply or 5 V USB brick. A 9 V alkaline is flat in about two hours
Ventilation Jars open, room ventilated — these cells make hydrogen all night

If the reveal failed

It happens, usually because a battery died or a clip came loose. Options, in order of preference:

  1. Run a fast cell live. Wire a fresh rusty nail at the highest safe current at the start of class and come back to it at the 60-minute mark. An hour at 0.5 A visibly changes a lightly rusted nail.
  2. Have a prepared example. Keep one successfully derusted nail from your pilot run, sealed in a bag. Show it, and be straightforward that it is from the pilot.
  3. Teach the failure. Ask the class to diagnose it: dead battery, loose clip, reversed polarity, too little electrolyte. Then check the anode — if it is heavily eaten, current definitely flowed, and the question becomes where the effect went. This is a genuinely good fifteen minutes and students respect it more than a smooth demo.

Whatever happens, write down what went wrong in shared/teaching-log.md while it is fresh.

Before class (instructor)

  • [ ] Mix 5–10% saltwater and label it "CORROSION DEMOS ONLY — NOT FOR ELECTROLYSIS OR DERUSTING"
  • [ ] Physically separate the saltwater from anything left over from Session 4
  • [ ] Sand all metal strips to bare metal, especially the aluminium, and keep them dry until use
  • [ ] Confirm every multimeter has a working mA range — Part B3 needs it, and a blown fuse reads zero
  • [ ] Pilot the metal pairs yourself and record what your saltwater actually gives; the numbers in instructor.md are typical, not guaranteed
  • [ ] Check the corrosion jars: are the metal contacts still firm? A slipped wire makes jar B look like jar A
  • [ ] Print E° tables with the predictions column blank
  • [ ] Print capstone worksheets and week-comparison tables
  • [ ] Locate photos of a ship's hull anode and a water-heater rod

Multi-day setup this session depends on

The corrosion jars need four to five days to develop. They belong at the end of Session 1, not here — see shared/multi-day-setup.md.

Jar Setup
A Iron nail alone in saltwater
B Iron nail in firm contact with copper
C Iron nail in firm contact with zinc, or a galvanized nail

Fill each jar half full so the metals cross the waterline. Keep them where students can look at them daily — the running commentary through the week is half the value.

If the jars were not started: set them up today anyway as a demonstration of method, and use the photograph fallback for the results. Then put a note in the teaching log so next year's Day 1 includes them.

Troubleshooting

Issue Cause Fix
Treated nail looks like the control Power died, clip loose, or too little time See the fallback options above
Nail is worse than when it started Polarity reversed — it was the anode Excellent teaching moment; show the class the wiring
Bath smells of chlorine Salt was used Stop, ventilate, dispose, remake with washing soda
Metal-pair voltage near zero Metals touching in solution, or an oxide layer Separate them; sand both
Aluminium reads lower than iron Expected — passive oxide film This is the lesson, not a fault
Voltages well below prediction Non-standard conditions; oxygen reduction at the cathode Expected. See instructor.md
Short-circuit current reads zero Meter not on mA, or a blown fuse Check the fuse
Jar B looks identical to jar A Contact slipped — no electron path Re-clamp; note it for next year
Jars barely rusted at all Started too late, or filled too full Use the fallback photo; start jars on Day 1 next year

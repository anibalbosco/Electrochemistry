Session 3 — Preparation

Session 3

The three things that decide whether this session works

  1. The bath is 0.05 M AgNO₃ in 0.2 M KNO₃, not dilute silver nitrate alone. The silver concentration sets the maximum current the cell can sustain; the nitrate makes the solution conduct.
  2. A 1 kΩ resistor goes in series at every station. It sets the current, so all benches match and nobody has to chase a target by fiddling with electrode depth.
  3. Live entry. The object is powered before it touches the liquid and stays powered until it leaves. Otherwise copper displaces silver directly and the coating will not adhere.

Before class (instructor)

Solution

  • [ ] Chloride-check the KNO₃ against AgNO₃ on a watch glass — clear, not milky
  • [ ] Mix the bath with distilled water: dissolve KNO₃ first, then AgNO₃
  • [ ] Enough for all groups at 30–40 mL each, plus 20%
  • [ ] Label with concentration and date; store dark
  • [ ] Confirm the silver waste protocol with the facility

Pilot — do this properly, it takes fifteen minutes

  • [ ] Plate one object end to end and record current, time, and appearance
  • [ ] Confirm the 1 kΩ resistor gives about 2 mA with your 3 V packs
  • [ ] Confirm the current stays flat across the run
  • [ ] Practise live entry yourself before teaching it
  • [ ] Run a control coin with no power for the same time and keep it — it is your demonstration piece
  • [ ] Measure start and end pH and check them against the charge
  • [ ] Photograph both coins side by side

Hardware

  • [ ] 1 kΩ resistors, one per station plus spares
  • [ ] Clean graphite anodes
  • [ ] Multimeter fuses checked — a blown mA fuse reads zero and looks like "no reaction"
  • [ ] Two matched, cleaned coins per group
  • [ ] Print pre-lab worksheets (lecture.md §5) and station cards

AgNO₃ handling

  • Gloves when preparing; ventilated area
  • Label everything; store away from organics and light
  • Have the ammonia warning ready to say out loud — see the safety section in experiment.md

Day-of setup

  • [ ] Pre-measured bath volumes at each station, to reduce spills
  • [ ] Resistors already inserted in the leads, so nobody forgets
  • [ ] Goggles enforced at the door
  • [ ] Silver waste container visible and labeled
  • [ ] Worked example on the board before students start
  • [ ] Control-coin dishes at each bench
  • [ ] Last year's dendrite photo displayed

Common failure modes

Issue Prevention
Coating rubs off Object entered the bath unpowered. Station a TA at each bench for the first entry
Grey fluffy deposit Current density too high. Check the resistor is actually in circuit and the area estimate is right
Bath cloudy from the start Chloride — tap water or contaminated nitrate. Chloride-check beforehand
Every bench a different current Resistor missing, or someone set a voltage instead
Meter reads zero Blown mA fuse, or not in series
Calculations stall Groups that skipped the §5 pre-lab gate. Enforce it
pH reading drifts or looks wrong Silver attacks glass electrodes with KCl references. Calibrate immediately before, rinse well after, or use paper
Someone proposes adding ammonia Pre-empt it in the safety brief. Ammoniacal silver forms explosive solids on standing

Timing note

Calculations take longer than expected. Have the worked example on the board as a check, and consider a spreadsheet template for groups that finish the arithmetic early — they can recompute for a different current and see how thickness scales.
